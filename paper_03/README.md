Seasonal and nutritional drivers of the foraging strategies of a wild frugivorous-faunivorous primate

Data availability

Raw data are stored in a private repository and are available upon request to the authors.

Abstract: Reaching feeding resources drives primates’ movement decisions, yet the factors shaping daily foraging strategies remain unclear, especially in altered forest fragments. We investigated how spatial distribution, seasonal resource availability, and fruit nutritional value influenced the foraging strategies of black lion tamarins (Leontopithecus chrysopygus, BLTs) in a 100-ha seasonal Atlantic Forest fragment. We predicted BLTs would consume more frequently the most abundant and energy-rich fruits, increase prey foraging in the dry season, when invertebrate biomass is lower, and consume high-energy fruits early in the day, after long periods of fasting. Over two three-months periods between 2022 and 2023, we sampled one habituated BLT group, recording behavior every five minutes, sampled fruiting tree phenology and invertebrate biomass across the group’s home range during the study periods, and estimated fruit nutritional value. We found that BLTs visited more frequently the most energy-rich fruits but not necessarily the most abundant. Frugivory was more likely in the morning during the rainy season but not in the dry. Prey foraging increased throughout the day in the dry season but peaked in the morning and afternoon in the rainy season. High-energy fruit consumption was prioritized early in the day. Hence, by combining behavioral observations with detailed measurements of fruit phenology, invertebrate biomass, and fruit nutritional content, we provided novel insights into the complex interplay between spatio-temporal variation in resource availability and resource nutritional quality affecting daily feeding decisions of a neotropical primate living in anthropized seasonal forest fragments.
